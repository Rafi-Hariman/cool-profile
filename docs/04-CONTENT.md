# Content Strategy

## Tone

Professional. Human. Confident. Clear. Technical without unnecessary jargon.

---

# Avoid

"Passionate developer", "Tech enthusiast", "Code ninja", "Pixel
perfectionist", "Turning coffee into code", generic motivational statements.

---

# Identity Block (sidebar)

Structure:

- Name
- Role (Frontend Engineer)
- One-line value proposition
- Availability

The final text must use the real user's information.

---

# About

Explain:

- Who I am
- What I specialize in
- What I enjoy building
- What engineering principles matter to me

---

# Experience

Use factual information only.

Never fabricate company, position, dates, metrics, or responsibilities.

Sample entries in `data/experience.ts` are marked `SAMPLE` and must be
replaced before the site represents real history.

---

# Projects

Every project needs: title, one-line summary, problem, solution, role,
technology, challenges, result, links.

Project data lives in `data/projects.ts`. Sample entries are marked
`SAMPLE` and must be replaced with real projects.

---

# Metrics

Metrics must be real.

Never invent performance improvements, revenue, user counts, percentages,
or conversion rates.

If real data is unavailable, describe qualitative impact instead.

(Consequence: fabricated figures such as "8+ years shipping", "50+
interfaces in production" were removed during the refactor. Add real
numbers back only when they are true.)

---

# Project Writing Formula

Problem: What was difficult?
Action: What did I build?
Reasoning: Why did I choose this solution?
Result: What changed?
Learning: What did I learn?

---

# Writing

Articles should demonstrate technical thinking. Good topics: frontend
architecture, performance, accessibility, TypeScript, React, Next.js,
design systems, testing, debugging, real project lessons.

`data/writing.ts` holds entries marked `SAMPLE` until real articles exist.
The Writing section renders only when the list is non-empty.

---

# Personal Brand

The portfolio should communicate:

"I am an engineer who understands users, products, and systems."

Not simply:

"I know many technologies."
